# AI video and AI school learning platforms, 2025–2026

## Which companies/products generate AI video lessons for schools or homeschool?

### Takeaway
The clearest current K–12 lesson-to-video product is **ibl.ai Agentic Video**; **AICoTeach** is another school-specific avatar-video product, focused on Hong Kong. **HeyGen, Synthesia, Fliki, and KreadoAI** generate avatar-led or narrated education video but are horizontal creation tools, not adaptive K–12 curricula. Most prominent AI-learning products in the prompt—Synthesis, Khanmigo, SchoolAI, Alpha/TimeBack, Outschool, MagicSchool, Diffit, and Conker—do **not** publicly claim to generate a personalized teacher video for each learner.

### Cited Findings
- **ibl.ai Agentic Video — direct K–12 video-generation match.** It turns a script, lesson plan, or outline into age-appropriate video with an AI instructor; can draft scenes, storyboards, animations, and callouts; can use Veo/Sora for generated visual sequences; and publishes through Google Classroom or district LMSs using LTI 1.3. It also advertises assessments with grade passback and student analytics. The ICP is districts and schools, and public pricing is not shown. — [ibl.ai K–12 Agentic Video](https://ibl.ai/product/agentic-video/k12)
- **AICoTeach — direct school avatar-video match, geographically narrow.** The Hong Kong-oriented product says schools can create avatar-teacher lessons in 15 minutes, in 140+ languages with lip sync and subtitles. It combines these with adaptive branching assessments, auto-grading, parent progress tools, low-bandwidth/offline delivery, and PDPO compliance. No public price was found. — [AICoTeach](https://aicoteach.com/)
- **HeyGen — real video generation, not a learning system.** Its course workflow drafts a short script from a lesson title and key points, renders a personal or stock avatar in the instructor’s voice, and exports MP4 and captions for upload to any LMS. The teacher approves the script before rendering; the same lecture can be translated without reshooting. HeyGen does not claim to diagnose a child, sequence curriculum, or evaluate mastery. — [HeyGen course creation](https://www.heygen.com/course-creation)
- HeyGen’s current tiers are Free ($0; 3 watermarked videos/month, up to 1 minute, 30+ languages), Creator ($29/month or $24/month annual; 600 credits, custom avatar/voice, 1080p, 175+ languages/dialects), Pro ($49/month; 1,000 credits, videos up to 30 minutes, 4K), and Business ($149/month plus $20/additional seat; videos up to 60 minutes). Its ICP is creators and teams rather than K–12 students. — [HeyGen pricing](https://www.heygen.com/pricing)
- **Synthesia — real avatar-video generation, primarily enterprise L&D.** It provides AI avatars, multilingual voices, an AI video assistant, PowerPoint import, captions, screen recording, and MP4 output. Its SCORM 1.2/2004 export can put videos in an LMS and report standard completion/status data, but SCORM export is Enterprise-only. — [Synthesia pricing](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing); [Synthesia SCORM guide](https://www.synthesia.io/post/scorm)
- Synthesia’s live pricing page has moved to a credit model: Basic is free, a paid individual tier starts at $19/month, Business is $99/month ($79/month annual), and Enterprise is custom. Older 2025 articles that quote Free/Starter $29/Creator $89 describe a previous plan structure and should not be used as current pricing. — [Synthesia pricing](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing)
- **Fliki — education-oriented horizontal generator.** Fliki says it converts lesson plans, lectures, and outlines into narrated K–12 videos with visuals, captions, quiz checkpoints, and a stock or cloned teacher avatar; it can localize into 80+ languages and export MP4/SRT for common LMS/course platforms. Its site claims audience controls can tune vocabulary and pacing for K–12, but there is no evidence on the cited page that this tuning responds to an individual learner’s measured mastery. — [Fliki education](https://fliki.ai/use-cases/education-and-e-learning)
- **KreadoAI — horizontal avatar lesson generator.** Teachers or institutions select/upload an avatar, select a voice, enter a script, customize the layout, and export the result. It supports subject-specific teacher avatars but does not claim adaptive sequencing or mastery measurement on its education page. — [KreadoAI education](https://www.kreadoai.com/education)
- **Primer Institute — AI curriculum with video as a curated component, not generated teacher video.** Its FAQ says lesson figures are programmatically drawn, optional illustrations may be AI-generated, but the lesson’s Video step is an embedded YouTube video chosen for the lesson. Parents can disable external videos and rate whether a video fit. Thus it generates/adapts the surrounding lesson, not the instructional video itself. — [Primer Institute FAQ](https://primer.institute/faq/)
- The **Primer Institute AI curriculum should not be conflated with Primer microschools**. In a March 2025 profile of the microschool company, Primer’s founder said AI tutors were not yet ready for prime time and that most AI/software work was administrative; instruction still combined live leaders, software, and virtual tutors. — [Not Boring, “Primer: From Software to Schools,” Mar. 5, 2025](https://www.notboring.co/p/primer-from-software-to-schools)
- **NextGen Schooling / NGEdu — homeschool system with videos, but public evidence of video generation is weak.** The Australian K–12 product advertises curriculum-aligned interactive videos, prepared daily lessons, AI tutors, learning games, quizzes, parent dashboards, and AI performance reports. Its public claims do not establish whether its videos are dynamically generated, pre-produced, or simply selected from a library. — [NGEdu launch announcement](https://www.skillihire.com/blog/ng-edu-ai-homeschooling-australia-launch); [NextGen Schooling site](https://www.ngedu.com.au/)
- **Teachable — not an AI video generator.** Teachable’s AI drafts curricula, course content, sales pages, and quizzes; its video AI transcribes and translates videos the creator uploads. Current plans start at $39/month monthly ($29/month annual), with AI creation included; free subtitle/translation generation starts on Growth ($189 monthly/$139 annual). — [Teachable AI hub](https://www.teachable.com/ai-hub); [Teachable pricing](https://www.teachable.com/pricing); [June 2025 plan changes](https://support.teachable.com/en/articles/11682410-new-teachable-plans-in-june-2025)
- **Outschool — live/self-paced human-teacher marketplace, not AI-generated video lessons.** Its 2025 AI features match learners to classes/teachers and generate post-class summaries from text transcripts. Outschool explicitly frames AI as enhancing, not replacing, teachers. — [Outschool AI family support, May 27, 2025](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/11465844-how-ai-enhances-your-family-s-outschool-experience)
- **Synthesis Tutor — adaptive interactive math, not generated video.** It teaches K–5 math through interactive, multisensory activities and tailored guidance. Its public product pages do not claim avatar or lesson-video generation. — [Synthesis about/product page](https://www.synthesis.com/about); [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)
- **Khanmigo — Socratic text tutor layered on Khan Academy content, not a video generator.** It provides personalized help on each Khan Academy exercise, article, or existing video and can discuss outside homework; the cited product material does not say Khanmigo creates new lesson videos. — [Khan Academy Districts](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/)
- **SchoolAI — conversational Spaces, not generated video.** Teachers create customizable AI learning environments that tailor examples, reading level, feedback, and difficulty; no video-generation feature appears on the cited Spaces/product pages. — [SchoolAI personalization](https://schoolai.com/blog/personalize-at-scale-with-schoolai); [SchoolAI Spaces](https://schoolai.com/products/spaces)
- **MagicSchool, Diffit, and Conker are teacher production/assessment tools, not AI video schools.** MagicSchool offers teacher/student generators and district controls; Diffit adapts readings/resources and can derive materials from a YouTube transcript; Conker generates and grades quizzes. None of the cited pages claims to render an AI teacher video. — [MagicSchool pricing/features](https://www.magicschool.ai/pricing); [Diffit pricing/features](https://web.diffit.me/pricing); [University of Cincinnati Conker guide](https://guides.libraries.uc.edu/ai-education/ca)

### Inferences
- The market is not yet a single end-to-end category. **Video generators** usually stop at production/export, while **adaptive-learning systems** usually render interactive text, questions, manipulatives, or existing media rather than generating per-student video. ibl.ai and AICoTeach are notable because they put video production and school delivery/assessment in one offer. — [ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/product/agentic-video/k12); [AICoTeach](https://aicoteach.com/)
- A product saying “AI-powered video learning” should not be assumed to generate video. Primer Institute and NGEdu illustrate the distinction between AI in the learning path and video as one media asset. — [Primer Institute FAQ](https://primer.institute/faq/); [NGEdu launch](https://www.skillihire.com/blog/ng-edu-ai-homeschooling-australia-launch)

### Gaps
- No public source found documents a mature K–12 product that continuously generates a unique avatar-led video lesson for each child from that child’s longitudinal mastery history, then experimentally selects the best teaching method.
- Public pricing was not found for ibl.ai Agentic Video, AICoTeach, or NGEdu beyond NGEdu’s advertised $1 AUD seven-day launch trial. — [NextGen launch offer](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nextgen-schooling-home-school-australia_nextgenschooling-homeschoolingaustralia-activity-7467556126730129408-IHwt)

## What do they personalize today?

### Takeaway
Current personalization is strongest in **topic/interest, reading level, next-question difficulty, pacing, language, and avatar/voice selection**. These controls often belong to different product types: adaptive tutors personalize the learning path, while avatar-video tools personalize the presenter and language. Few products combine both.

### Cited Findings
- **Synthesis:** adapts K–5 math in real time, fills gaps, advances after mastery, provides tailored guidance on every problem, supports English and Spanish, and automatically reads aloud for children under seven. The family product covers ages 5–11; school use includes classrooms, homeschool pods, and districts. — [Synthesis about/product page](https://www.synthesis.com/about)
- **Primer Institute:** intake collects interests, learning preferences, current subjects, challenges, goals, standards alignment, and safety settings. Lessons and worksheets are adaptive; parents can request pacing changes, custom lessons, foreign languages, and custom topics through a named AI teacher. Accessibility includes read-aloud and dyslexia-friendly mode. — [Primer videos](https://primer.institute/videos/); [Primer FAQ](https://primer.institute/faq/)
- Primer Institute’s plans are Focus at $20/student/month for one core subject (500 credits and three custom lessons monthly) and Unlimited at $40/student/month for all K–12 subjects plus foreign-language, supportive, and custom-topic lessons (1,000 credits). Annual billing is $216/$432 and both have a 14-day trial. The ICP is homeschool curriculum, single-subject tutoring alongside school, and learners who need individually shaped lessons. — [Primer FAQ](https://primer.institute/faq/)
- **SchoolAI:** Spaces can ask about interests and tailor examples, offer learner-selected paths, change Lexile/vocabulary level, use voice or bilingual options, reteach gaps with a new explanation, and scale difficulty based on performance. Teachers can also set grade, subject, and teaching style so responses follow their instructional approach. — [SchoolAI personalization](https://schoolai.com/blog/personalize-at-scale-with-schoolai); [SchoolAI Spaces](https://schoolai.com/products/spaces)
- **SchoolAI pricing/ICP:** the free trial permits five Space launches/year; Pro adds unlimited sessions, rostering, insights, smart grouping, LMS integration, and support; Scale adds student portals, uploads, Canvas-embedded Sidekick, student-level insights, and district learning frameworks. Paid prices are quote-based. The product targets teachers, schools, and districts, not direct homeschool families. — [SchoolAI pricing](https://schoolai.com/pricing)
- **Khanmigo:** personalizes conversational help within Khan Academy content rather than creating a new curriculum or video. District Learning Paths can use NWEA MAP scores to personalize paths in grades 3–8 math, Algebra 1, and Geometry. Individuals pay $4/month or $44/year; teacher tools are free; the district package with Khanmigo is $15/student with a 250-license minimum, U.S.-only, and requires Clever/ClassLink. — [Khanmigo pricing](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing); [Khan Academy Districts](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/)
- **Alpha/TimeBack:** the company describes personalized, mastery-based paths, real-time skill tracking, adaptive tutoring, annual goals broken into visible tasks, and motivation/XP systems. TimeBack was still beta during Alpha’s 2025–26 transition. — [Alpha introducing TimeBack](https://alpha.school/blog/introducing-timeback-the-next-evolution-of-alphas-model/)
- An observer of Alpha’s app stack reported that AlphaRead, AlphaWrite, AlphaLearn, AlphaMath Fluency, and TeachTales used AI-generated content for personalized paths, but 2025 delivery used static, human-checked pre-generated content, with dynamic real-time generation planned for 2026. This is third-party reporting, not a product specification. — [Austin Scholar, “The Alpha App Stack”](https://austinscholar.substack.com/p/austin-scholar-177-the-alpha-app)
- Alpha’s partner-school product was quoted at $10,000/student/year including a trained guide, while Alpha’s own blog says the platform was still beta. A 2026 third-party report says Alpha Anywhere pricing around $833/month was parent-reported, not officially published. — [Future of Education partner-school explanation](https://futureofeducation.substack.com/p/ask-me-anything-9-what-about-alphas); [Alpha Anywhere pricing caveat](https://www.aifunlab.io/learn/alpha-anywhere-homeschool-alternatives)
- **Outschool:** personalizes class discovery by learner needs/interests and recommends suitable teachers; its free human Concierge also considers age/grade, goals, schedule, live versus self-paced preference, group size, teaching style, and desired course duration. This personalizes selection, not the content inside a class in real time. — [Outschool AI](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/11465844-how-ai-enhances-your-family-s-outschool-experience); [Outschool Concierge](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/11690814-welcome-to-outschool-concierge)
- **HeyGen:** personalizes stock versus custom teacher avatar, cloned voice, script, accent/tone/speed, language/dialect, visual template, and brand. These are author-controlled variants, not learner-model-driven adaptation. — [HeyGen course creation](https://www.heygen.com/course-creation); [HeyGen pricing](https://www.heygen.com/pricing)
- **Synthesia:** personalizes avatar, voice/language, templates, imported slides/media, and enterprise branding; it localizes a fixed instructional message rather than adapting the pedagogy to a measured student. — [Synthesia pricing](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing)
- **MagicSchool:** teacher-side differentiation includes text leveling, translation, lesson/assessment/IEP generation, and custom student chatbots. Free serves individual exploration; Plus is $12.99/month or $99.96/year; district Enterprise is custom. — [MagicSchool pricing](https://www.magicschool.ai/pricing)
- **Diffit:** adapts source material to reading level and creates summaries, vocabulary, questions, graphic organizers, and standards/DOK/MTSS-aligned resources. The free tier has limits; school pricing is annual and enrollment-based; public list pricing is not shown on the current page. — [Diffit pricing](https://web.diffit.me/pricing)
- **Conker:** adjusts generated assessments by topic, standards, question type, and source material and supports read-aloud, LMS/Google Forms, grading, and progress tracking. Public university guidance lists Free, $3.99/month, $5.99/month, and custom school plans, but also warns generated answers may be inaccurate and need human review. — [University of Cincinnati Conker guide](https://guides.libraries.uc.edu/ai-education/ca)
- **NGEdu:** vendor claims include strengths/weaknesses, pace, behavioral/cognitive patterns, automatic difficulty adjustment, micro-lesson recommendations, AI tutors, and family progress reports. These claims come from launch/marketing material, and no independent implementation evidence was found. — [NGEdu launch](https://www.skillihire.com/blog/ng-edu-ai-homeschooling-australia-launch); [NextGen launch offer](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nextgen-schooling-home-school-australia_nextgenschooling-homeschoolingaustralia-activity-7467556126730129408-IHwt)

### Inferences
- “Personalized” has at least four non-equivalent meanings in this market: **media localization** (avatar/language), **teacher-authored differentiation** (reading level/material variant), **session adaptation** (next hint/question), and **longitudinal pathing** (placement/mastery path). Vendor comparisons should label which layer is present. — [HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/course-creation); [SchoolAI](https://schoolai.com/blog/personalize-at-scale-with-schoolai); [Khan Academy Districts](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/)
- Avatar and language selection are usually production settings. No cited avatar-video vendor shows that the system autonomously chooses a teacher avatar, explanation style, or language because that choice previously improved a particular student’s retention.

### Gaps
- Public sources rarely specify the learner-model variables, adaptation algorithm, error rates, or whether “learning style” inputs improve outcomes.
- Alpha/TimeBack and NGEdu expose unusually broad personalization claims, but public technical documentation and independent validation are thin. — [Alpha TimeBack](https://alpha.school/blog/introducing-timeback-the-next-evolution-of-alphas-model/); [NGEdu launch](https://www.skillihire.com/blog/ng-edu-ai-homeschooling-australia-launch)

## Do they evaluate delayed mastery or teaching method, or only completion and quizzes?

### Takeaway
Most products measure **immediate performance**: completion, quiz/exit-ticket results, current conversation evidence, or standardized-test growth. Some use repeated practice or mastery gates, but public evidence of **scheduled delayed retrieval tests** is scarce. No reviewed product publicly demonstrates a rigorous system that compares teaching methods per learner and attributes later retention to the method used.

### Cited Findings
- **Synthesis** says every lesson contains micro-assessments and that the tutor adapts in real time, fills gaps, and moves ahead after mastery. This supports immediate/formative mastery. Its public product page does not document delayed retention checks or method-effect experiments. — [Synthesis about/product page](https://www.synthesis.com/about)
- A June 2026 user critique says Synthesis’s main lesson flow has little spaced review and often accepts one or two successful demonstrations before moving on. This is anecdotal, not a controlled audit, but it is the only detailed public source found that directly addresses delayed review. Synthesis separately offers adaptive flashcards described by a third party as spaced repetition; that should not be assumed to characterize the core Tutor. — [Matthew Daw critique](https://medium.com/@mattdaw7/improving-synthesis-tutoring-a108c7d6fc4c); [AI Made Simple overview](https://ai-made.beehiiv.com/p/synthesis-tutor-the-ai-maths-tutor-born-inside-spacex)
- **Primer Institute** says lessons contain practice and an exit ticket, future lessons reinforce missed answers and skip understood material, and parents receive weekly summaries. This implies longitudinal use of errors, but the FAQ does not publish a delayed-mastery protocol, retention interval, or method-attribution logic. — [Primer FAQ](https://primer.institute/faq/)
- **SchoolAI** evaluates understanding during student conversations, provides rubric-based feedback, flags gaps/frustration in real time, creates exit tickets, and groups students by demonstrated misconceptions. This is richer than completion tracking, but primarily formative and immediate; no delayed-retest method is described. — [SchoolAI personalization](https://schoolai.com/blog/personalize-at-scale-with-schoolai); [SchoolAI dashboard](https://schoolai.com/blog/how-to-use-schoolai-teacher-dashboard-understand-student-needs-real-time)
- **Khanmigo/Khan Academy** can summarize student work and identify areas for support, while district Learning Paths use MAP Growth scores to set personalized routes. MAP provides longitudinal external checkpoints, but the product sources do not say Khanmigo isolates which Socratic prompt or explanation caused later mastery. — [Khanmigo pricing/features](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing); [Khan Academy Districts](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/)
- **Alpha/TimeBack** uses mastery-based app progression, real-time skill tracking, and NWEA MAP growth testing. Alpha’s own 2024–25 NWEA report contains observed fall-to-spring growth by grade, so it does evaluate change over months rather than only completion. — [Alpha TimeBack](https://alpha.school/blog/introducing-timeback-the-next-evolution-of-alphas-model/); [Alpha 2024–25 NWEA report](https://go.alpha.school/hubfs/MAP%20Results%20-%2024%2025/2025%20NWEA%20MAP%20results.pdf)
- Alpha’s headline “2x” claim is not independently validated. An external analysis argues an Alpha white paper averaged the “Observed Growth SE” column rather than “Observed Growth,” estimates lower gains, and notes the 2024–25 sample was only 154 PK–8 learners. Another independent educator notes tuition/selectivity create strong selection effects and says available data cannot definitively attribute faster learning to Alpha’s model. Treat Alpha’s outcome claims as vendor claims, not causal evidence. — [Base Classes critique](https://baseclasses.com/p/the-alpha-math-problem); [Justin Baeder Q&A](https://www.justinbaeder.com/p/alpha-school-q-and-a)
- **Outschool** produces class summaries from transcripts and may help teachers generate assessments/reports, but its public AI page does not claim longitudinal mastery diagnosis. — [Outschool AI](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/11465844-how-ai-enhances-your-family-s-outschool-experience)
- **ibl.ai** and **AICoTeach** attach quizzes/adaptive assessments to generated video, while Synthesia’s SCORM output can support LMS completion/status reporting. None of their cited pages describes delayed retention testing or causal comparison of avatar, explanation, or pacing variants. — [ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/product/agentic-video/k12); [AICoTeach](https://aicoteach.com/); [Synthesia SCORM](https://www.synthesia.io/post/scorm)
- **MagicSchool, Diffit, and Conker** generate differentiated materials or assessments; they help a teacher measure learners but are not themselves documented as maintaining a longitudinal mastery model. — [MagicSchool pricing/features](https://www.magicschool.ai/pricing); [Diffit pricing/features](https://web.diffit.me/pricing); [University of Cincinnati Conker guide](https://guides.libraries.uc.edu/ai-education/ca)
- **NGEdu** claims mastery gates, real-time checks, weak-concept detection, difficulty changes, and weekly recommendations, but no public source found defines what counts as mastery or whether skills are retested after delay. — [NGEdu features page](https://www.ngedu.com.au/features); [NGEdu launch](https://www.skillihire.com/blog/ng-edu-ai-homeschooling-australia-launch)

### Inferences
- “Mastery-based” should not be equated with durable mastery. A gate based on an immediate correct response measures current performance; durable learning requires later retrieval under an explicit interval and ideally transfer to a new context.
- The closest products get to teaching-method evaluation is choosing a different explanation after an error or letting a teacher inspect which interaction revealed a misconception. That is responsive instruction, not controlled comparison of methods.
- Alpha’s periodic MAP testing is the strongest delayed outcome measure among the reviewed AI-school models, but it evaluates total-program growth, not which AI lesson, medium, avatar, or pedagogy worked.

### Gaps
- No reviewed product publicly reports retention curves, scheduled delayed retrieval intervals, transfer tasks, or per-method causal uplift.
- No reviewed product publishes whether it learns that a specific child retains more from diagrams versus avatar explanation versus manipulatives and then verifies that preference on later assessments.

## Pricing and ideal customer profile

### Takeaway
Pricing spans inexpensive family subscriptions and creator tools to high-cost school programs. The most important buying distinction is whether the customer needs **content production**, **adaptive tutoring**, **teacher workflow**, or a **school replacement**.

### Cited Findings
- **Family/homeschool tutoring:** Synthesis is $29/month or $119/year for up to seven children; its K–5 math ICP is families, homeschoolers, and elementary schools. A higher Pro tier is shown at $239/year with placement checks, personalized planning, and summaries. — [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)
- **AI-generated homeschool curriculum:** Primer Institute is $20/student/month for one subject or $40/student/month for all K–12 subjects, with 10% annual savings and a 14-day trial. — [Primer FAQ](https://primer.institute/faq/)
- **General learner/tutor:** Khanmigo is $4/month or $44/year for a U.S. learner/parent; teacher tools are free. Districts pay $15/student for Khanmigo plus partnership features, with a 250-seat minimum and U.S./rostering restrictions. — [Khanmigo pricing](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing); [Khan Academy Districts](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/)
- **Premium school replacement:** Alpha partner-school software/model was quoted at $10,000/student/year including a trained guide; Alpha’s full campus model has been reported at much higher tuition, and Alpha Anywhere’s roughly $833/month figure is third-party/parent-reported rather than an official current list price. — [Future of Education](https://futureofeducation.substack.com/p/ask-me-anything-9-what-about-alphas); [Alpha Anywhere caveat](https://www.aifunlab.io/learn/alpha-anywhere-homeschool-alternatives)
- **Video production:** HeyGen ranges from free to $29 Creator, $49 Pro, and $149+seat Business per month; Synthesia ranges from free to $19 individual and $99 Business monthly, with custom Enterprise; ibl.ai and AICoTeach require sales contact/no public quote. These tools target teachers, content teams, districts, or L&D producers rather than autonomous child users. — [HeyGen pricing](https://www.heygen.com/pricing); [Synthesia pricing](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing); [ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/product/agentic-video/k12); [AICoTeach](https://aicoteach.com/)
- **Teacher workflow:** MagicSchool is free or $12.99/month ($99.96/year) for individuals, with custom Enterprise; Diffit has free teacher access and enrollment-tiered school licenses; Conker has free, $3.99/month, $5.99/month, and custom school tiers. — [MagicSchool pricing](https://www.magicschool.ai/pricing); [Diffit pricing](https://web.diffit.me/pricing); [University of Cincinnati Conker guide](https://guides.libraries.uc.edu/ai-education/ca)
- **SchoolAI:** free trial for five launches; Pro and Scale are quote-based and aim at school/district deployment with rostering, LMS, dashboards, and governance. — [SchoolAI pricing](https://schoolai.com/pricing)
- **Creator course hosting:** Teachable starts at $39/month ($29/month annual) plus a 7.5% transaction fee on Starter; higher tiers target businesses selling courses, not K–12 mastery learning. — [Teachable pricing](https://www.teachable.com/pricing)
- **Outschool:** the relevant economic model is a marketplace of paid human-led classes rather than a single AI-school subscription; its AI features improve matching and visibility around those classes. — [Outschool AI](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/11465844-how-ai-enhances-your-family-s-outschool-experience)

### Inferences
- A direct competitor map should use four buckets: (1) AI video authoring, (2) adaptive student tutoring/curriculum, (3) teacher content/assessment workflow, and (4) full-school/homeschool operating model. Comparing all four on one “AI education” feature list obscures fundamentally different buyers and outcomes.
- The clearest whitespace is an end-to-end K–12 product that combines validated curriculum, dynamically generated teacher/avatar video, longitudinal mastery checks, method-level experimentation, parent/teacher controls, and school-safe governance.

### Gaps
- Enterprise and district vendors generally hide price, preventing a reliable per-student cost comparison.
- Current public pricing was not found for NGEdu, ibl.ai Agentic Video, or AICoTeach.
- Product claims and pricing are changing quickly; quotes should be rechecked before procurement, especially Synthesia, Alpha/TimeBack, and early-stage NGEdu.
