# AI Homeschool and Microschool Evaluation & Personalization (2024–2026)

## What evaluation loops and learner signals exist today?

### Takeaway
The dominant loop is diagnostic placement → app activity → immediate correctness feedback → mastery gate → next lesson/remediation, with progress exposed in student, staff, and parent dashboards. The richest public implementation is TimeBack/Alpha: it combines accuracy, active time, completion, XP, mastery state, “waste”/distraction, test history, and external MAP/state-test outcomes; Synthesis and Primer disclose simpler continuous-check and benchmark loops.

### Cited Findings
- **Alpha School / 2 Hour Learning / TimeBack:** Alpha’s student dashboard routes each learner to work at an appropriate level for remediation or acceleration, shows the next activity, and tracks core-skill achievement in real time for students, guides, and parents. This is level/pacing personalization rather than a common age-grade sequence. — [Alpha School](https://alpha.school/resources/how-alpha-two-hour-school-day-works/)
- TimeBack’s learning-system layer records periodic heartbeats for time spent, activity completion and results, updates mastery state, and sends the resulting events into analytics. Its documented signal set is XP earned, accuracy, time-to-mastery, waste percentage, and standardized-test scores; the desktop launcher also collects app/session context and attempts to distinguish active learning from idle, distracted, or multitasking time. — [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works)
- TimeBack explicitly separates **effort from mastery**: approximately 1 XP represents one minute of expected focused, productive work, while mastery is a separate state. It recommends XP only after verified learning, sets 80% accuracy for lessons/practice and 90% for quizzes/assessments, and discounts XP on repeated attempts. — [TimeBack: XP System](https://docs.platform.timeback.com/getting-started/xp-system)
- TimeBack’s closed loop links in-app activity to MAP Growth, state assessments, and SAT/AP outcomes. It can use missed standards to route students to “hole filling” remediation and later retry, but its own documentation cautions that availability and thresholds vary by workflow and that telemetry/correlation alone does not establish causation. — [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works)
- TimeBack’s parent materials expose daily/weekly XP, active learning time, average accuracy, lessons and quizzes with timestamps, subject/app usage, completed tests, current work relative to grade expectations, mastered levels, MAP RIT scores, and national/Alpha percentiles. Advancement requires 90% or higher on a level test. — [Alpha/TimeBack parent guide](https://support.alpha.school/api/v1/articles/134425/attachments/1949856/download)
- A detailed parent account describes immediate per-question feedback, grade-level mastery tests, missed questions returning for review, coaching calls after repeated stumbles, and parent views of daily, weekly, and overall progress. It also alleges click and eye-attention tracking; that last signal is not confirmed in the current TimeBack technical documentation and should be treated as anecdotal. — [ACX parent review archive](https://acxreviews.robennals.org/reviews/alpha-school)
- **Synthesis Tutor:** Synthesis says it embeds micro-assessments throughout every lesson, gives immediate tailored guidance on each problem, evaluates the learner continuously, fills detected knowledge gaps, and advances only after demonstrated mastery. Parent/teacher progress reports show what the child learned; the Pro tier adds placement checks, session summaries, weekly progress emails, and a personalized lesson planner. — [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about); [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)
- Synthesis describes its assessment as continuous rather than a one-time test, but its public pages reviewed here do not disclose mastery thresholds, item counts, a formal learner-model schema, or whether speed/hesitation is actually captured. — [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about)
- **Primer:** Every student begins with a nationally normed math/literacy assessment; students advance through concepts by mastery, families track weekly goals and progress, and formal benchmarks are administered three times per year. Students, teachers, and parents share one connected progress view. — [Primer Approach](https://primer.com/approach)
- Primer founder Ryan Delk said the starting test is NWEA MAP, an individual academic model is built from the result, and progress data then comes from “a variety of exit tickets.” Primer takes MAP three times yearly and compares those benchmark results with its projected learning velocity; classroom, campus, and network staff can drill down to daily student data. — [Michael B. Horn interview with Primer](https://michaelbhorn.substack.com/p/inside-primers-growing-network)
- Primer’s redesigned teacher dashboard displays daily/weekly goal completion and, for integrated apps such as Khan Academy, unit, mastery, time spent, and skills completed. Behavioral patterns can trigger “learning alerts,” after which a teacher can assign personalized growth areas for the next day. — [Everyday Industries Primer case study](https://everydayindustries.com/casestudy/ux-product-redesign-microschools-primer/)
- Primer’s parent app describes concept-level math/literacy mastery, progress toward individual goals, academic milestones, and direct communication with teachers, deliberately replacing letter grades with visible growth. — [Primer App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/primer/id1517900736)
- **Comparable products:** Omega School publishes a five-stage loop: prerequisite recall, instruction with worked examples and a three-level hint ladder, guided/independent practice, five “blind” questions with no hints or per-item feedback, and evidence written to a mastery map. Unaided correct streaks and a mastery check determine mastery. — [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring)
- Omega also claims a delayed loop: each topic receives a next-review date, today’s results determine a review quiz two weeks later, and a “give-up” returns later as a fresh variation. Wrong math answers are mapped to a known-misconception catalog and reported to parents with an interpretation and suggested response. — [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring)
- StarSpark describes an optional topic diagnostic, mastery checks, automatic review of older concepts, and event-level intervention when a student guesses rapidly, misses the same idea twice, or exhibits a misconception. It then pauses the assignment, reteaches, assigns targeted practice, returns the learner to the original work, and logs the event for the parent. — [StarSpark Homeschool](https://starspark.ai/homeschool); [StarSpark FAQ](https://starspark.ai/faq)
- AdaptEd claims a short misconception probe, a named misconception linked to an underlying concept, a custom visual/voice reteach, concept-specific game practice, a same-concept retest, and real-time parent dashboard/weekly summary. These are vendor claims from an early-stage product, not independent validation. — [AdaptEd](https://learnadapted.com/)

### Inferences
- Today’s most common “knowledge model” is a standards/skill mastery map updated by quiz outcomes, not a publicly documented probabilistic knowledge-tracing model. TimeBack exposes mastery state and cross-app events, Synthesis says it evaluates continuously, and Omega calls its representation a mastery map, but none of the reviewed public materials identifies Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, Deep Knowledge Tracing, model parameters, or confidence calibration. — [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works); [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about); [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring)
- Most loops optimize **proximal performance**—correctness, mastery-gate passage, task completion, and pace. TimeBack is unusual in also connecting activity to external standardized outcomes and explicitly distinguishing effort from progress, while Omega is unusual in publicly specifying a delayed review date. — [TimeBack: XP System](https://docs.platform.timeback.com/getting-started/xp-system); [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works); [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring)
- Dashboards are generally stronger at answering “what did the learner do, how accurately, and what is next?” than “which explanation caused durable understanding?” The exposed fields are dominated by time, accuracy, XP, skills, completion, mastery, goals, and benchmark growth. — [Alpha/TimeBack parent guide](https://support.alpha.school/api/v1/articles/134425/attachments/1949856/download); [Everyday Industries Primer case study](https://everydayindustries.com/casestudy/ux-product-redesign-microschools-primer/); [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)

### Gaps
- No reviewed vendor publishes item-level validity/reliability, false-mastery rates, learner-model calibration, or how many independent retrievals are required before a skill is considered stable.
- Public documentation does not show whether Alpha/TimeBack’s “waste detection” is used directly in student routing, only that it is collected and available for diagnosis/analytics.
- The reviewed Primer materials do not specify exact mastery thresholds, exit-ticket design, remediation rules, or how third-party app mastery scales are normalized.
- Synthesis does not publicly specify the precise signals behind “evaluates your child as they work”; claims in secondary reviews about hesitation, response speed, or reasoning-path analysis were not corroborated by Synthesis’s own pages.

## Where does personalization stop?

### Takeaway
For Alpha/TimeBack and Primer, public evidence is strongest for adapting placement, pace, lesson/app sequence, remediation, and goal intensity. Synthesis goes further at the lesson-interface level with immediate guidance and interactive representations, but public evidence still does not establish a robust causal policy that chooses among teaching methods based on each learner’s prior response.

### Cited Findings
- Alpha describes personalization as meeting each learner at the right level for remediation or acceleration, while the TimeBack platform provides placement, sequencing, mastery gates, spaced repetition, and remediation routing. TimeBack explicitly says subject-specific pedagogy and the learning experience/content are built by each app, not by the platform itself. — [Alpha School](https://alpha.school/resources/how-alpha-two-hour-school-day-works/); [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works); [TimeBack: Integration Levels](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/build-on-timeback/integration-levels)
- TimeBack’s native Level 2 integration provides placement data, PowerPath sequencing/mastery logic, QTI content, and a review-scheduling algorithm. Level 1 apps keep their own learning engines, diagnostics, and content logic, so “TimeBack-powered” does not imply one universal adaptive tutor or one consistent depth of personalization. — [TimeBack: Integration Levels](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/build-on-timeback/integration-levels)
- TimeBack can distinguish three operational failure patterns—low effort, wrong placement, and curriculum completion without external-score improvement—and its developer dashboard can compare app outcomes. Its docs frame instructional redesign as something developers evaluate and improve, not as an automatic per-student switch of pedagogical method. — [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works)
- Primer’s disclosed personalization begins from an NWEA-based academic model and updates daily progress/learning-velocity projections from exit tickets and app data. If a learner is projected not to catch up, staff set more aggressive goals; dashboard alerts prompt teacher feedback and next-day growth areas. The public mechanism is therefore partly algorithmic sequencing and partly human intervention. — [Michael B. Horn interview with Primer](https://michaelbhorn.substack.com/p/inside-primers-growing-network); [Everyday Industries Primer case study](https://everydayindustries.com/casestudy/ux-product-redesign-microschools-primer/)
- Synthesis says its tutor gives immediate, tailored guidance on each problem, adjusts in real time, uses interactive manipulatives and read-aloud, remembers patterns, identifies where a child is stuck, and guides step by step. These claims support adaptation within a lesson, but its primary pages do not explain whether the system chooses a different representation because a previous representation failed. — [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about); [Synthesis: Why Math Finally Clicks](https://www.synthesis.com/blog/why-math-finally-clicks-with-synthesis-tutor)
- An independent analysis of an earlier Synthesis demo found linked visual representations, limited branching, extra messages after errors or delay, and repeated final exercises, but characterized the sequence as mostly scripted and not a fine-grained intelligent tutoring system capable of diagnosing which conceptual atom failed. This is useful counterevidence, though it may not represent the 2026 product. — [Andy Matuschak’s Synthesis Tutor notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zN24T3P8FH7F73F6gBy4XCM)
- Alpha’s published 2024–25 NWEA report shows high school-level MAP growth, but this is observational school data, not an experiment isolating the effects of AI, mastery gating, guides, selection, or other model components. TimeBack itself warns that outcome correlation does not establish causation. — [Alpha 2024–25 NWEA report](https://go.alpha.school/hubfs/MAP%20Results%20-%2024%2025/2025%20NWEA%20MAP%20results.pdf); [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works)
- Independent critics note selection effects from tuition/enrollment and the absence of definitive comparative evidence; another analysis identifies a prior Alpha “2x” calculation that used NWEA’s observed-growth standard-error column rather than observed growth. These critiques do not show Alpha’s students fail to learn, but they make causal and multiplier claims less secure than the dashboard mechanics themselves. — [Justin Baeder Q&A](https://www.justinbaeder.com/p/alpha-school-q-and-a); [Luke Shepard analysis](https://baseclasses.com/p/the-alpha-math-problem)

### Inferences
- For the core named platforms, the practical stopping point is usually **“select the right skill/activity and adjust pace/support”**, not **“infer which pedagogy works for this learner, test an alternative, and retain a personalized teaching policy.”** Alpha/TimeBack routes among apps and lessons while delegating pedagogy to apps; Primer combines individualized plans with teacher action; Synthesis discloses tailored guidance but not the decision policy behind it. — [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works); [Michael B. Horn interview with Primer](https://michaelbhorn.substack.com/p/inside-primers-growing-network); [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about)
- Cross-app outcome correlation could eventually support method selection—for example, learning which app or lesson design produces faster durable mastery for a subgroup—but the current TimeBack documentation describes developer analytics and content iteration, not automated N-of-1 pedagogical experimentation for each child. — [TimeBack: How It Works](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/how-it-works)
- Human guides remain a substantive part of the adaptation loop. Alpha guides coach/motivate and coaching calls can be triggered around difficulty; Primer teachers act on alerts and revise goals. These models should not be described as fully autonomous AI instruction. — [Alpha School](https://alpha.school/resources/how-alpha-two-hour-school-day-works/); [ACX parent review archive](https://acxreviews.robennals.org/reviews/alpha-school); [Everyday Industries Primer case study](https://everydayindustries.com/casestudy/ux-product-redesign-microschools-primer/)

### Gaps
- No named platform publishes an ablation study comparing topic sequencing alone against teaching-method adaptation.
- No public technical description was found of a student-specific policy that records “visual explanation failed, worked example succeeded” and preferentially uses that method on future concepts.
- No public evidence was found that parent dashboards expose method-level effectiveness, uncertainty, retention decay, or confidence that a learner’s mastery will transfer after a delay.
- Alpha’s causal outcome claims remain hard to separate from admissions/selection, human guides, motivation systems, app choice, and total program design.

## Which products adapt how content is taught, and what is still missing?

### Takeaway
There are credible public **claims** of method adaptation, but the clearest ones come from smaller or early-stage products rather than the major microschools. Omega specifies worked-example/hint escalation, delayed blind checks, and misconception labels; Lemonæ and AdaptEd claim misconception-specific visual reteaching; Synthesis uses multiple modalities and tailored guidance but does not clearly document method selection as a response policy. The major missing loop is longitudinal: diagnose a misconception, vary the instructional representation, test unaided performance after a delay, and learn which method produced durable transfer for that individual.

### Cited Findings
- TimeBack requires integrated apps to use retrieval practice, ≥90% mastery gates, spaced review, worked examples, immediate/elaborated feedback, contrastive examples, near-misses, and rapid error correction. These are platform-wide instructional design constraints, but they prescribe good content design rather than proving dynamic selection among methods for an individual learner. — [TimeBack: The Principles](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/principles); [TimeBack: Non-negotiables](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/concepts/non-negotiables)
- Omega publishes the most explicit full method-and-evaluation chain found: prerequisite recall; instruction through questions and worked examples; a hint ladder that restates, points to the concept, then shows a different worked example; unaided practice; a five-question blind assessment; misconception mapping; and a review quiz scheduled roughly two weeks later. — [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring)
- Lemonæ claims it identifies a learner’s misconception from mistakes and uses a knowledge graph to adapt synchronized visuals, voice, animation, worked examples, and interest-based contexts. However, the site also says the product is in early development/early access, so these are product-intent claims, not evidence of a mature deployed loop. — [Lemonæ](https://lemonae.ai/)
- AdaptEd claims it names a specific misconception, generates a roughly 90-second visual/voice whiteboard reteach around that exact gap, gives a concept-embodied game, and retests the concept before updating the parent dashboard. The public evidence is a vendor demonstration, with no independent outcome validation found. — [AdaptEd](https://learnadapted.com/)
- StarSpark claims to infer a misconception from work in progress, pause the original assignment, teach step by step with worked examples and quick checks, add targeted practice, then return the learner to the original task. It also says older concepts are automatically brought back before they fade. Its public pages do not detail delayed-test intervals or diagnostic accuracy. — [StarSpark Homeschool](https://starspark.ai/homeschool); [StarSpark FAQ](https://starspark.ai/faq)
- Synthesis clearly varies **modality**—interactive manipulatives, read-aloud, voice-speed controls, digital playgrounds, and visual models—and says guidance is tailored problem by problem. Its primary materials do not clearly claim that a measured failure causes a switch from, for example, story to diagram to worked example, nor do they publish a misconception taxonomy. — [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about); [Synthesis: Why Math Finally Clicks](https://www.synthesis.com/blog/why-math-finally-clicks-with-synthesis-tutor)
- Primer mentions “targeted instruction, adaptive tools, and direct teaching,” but its public detail centers on levels, goals, mastery, teacher insight, and benchmark growth rather than automated changes in explanation format. — [Primer Approach](https://primer.com/approach)

### Inferences
- A useful distinction is **multi-modal content** versus **method adaptation**. Offering visuals, voice, manipulatives, stories, or worked examples is not itself adaptive; method adaptation requires evidence that the system chooses or changes a representation because of a learner-specific diagnostic signal. Synthesis publicly establishes the former more strongly than the latter, while Lemonæ and AdaptEd explicitly claim the latter but lack mature independent evidence. — [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about); [Lemonæ](https://lemonae.ai/); [AdaptEd](https://learnadapted.com/)
- Delayed checks are rare in public product descriptions. TimeBack mandates spacing and offers a scheduling algorithm, StarSpark claims automatic review, and Omega explicitly says a topic can return in a review quiz two weeks later; most other disclosed loops retest immediately after reteaching, which measures short-term performance more than retention. — [TimeBack: Integration Levels](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/build-on-timeback/integration-levels); [StarSpark FAQ](https://starspark.ai/faq); [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring)
- Misconception diagnosis is also uneven. TimeBack emphasizes preventing misconceptions and routing missed standards, while Omega, Lemonæ, AdaptEd, and StarSpark explicitly claim to name or detect misconception patterns. The latter claims would be more decision-useful if vendors published taxonomies, confusion matrices, human-audit results, and examples of how diagnoses alter subsequent instruction. — [TimeBack: The Principles](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/principles); [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring); [Lemonæ](https://lemonae.ai/); [AdaptEd](https://learnadapted.com/); [StarSpark FAQ](https://starspark.ai/faq)
- The missing evaluation loop can be stated as: **observe error/reasoning → diagnose prerequisite or misconception with uncertainty → choose a contrasting teaching method → verify unaided near transfer → recheck after days/weeks → test far transfer → update both the mastery estimate and the learner’s method-response history.** Existing products publicly document pieces of this chain, but no reviewed platform documents the whole chain as a production, validated per-learner system. — [TimeBack: The Principles](https://docs.timeback.com/beta/about-timeback/principles); [Omega School tutoring](https://omega.foundation/programs/tutoring); [Synthesis: About](https://www.synthesis.com/about)

### Gaps
- No independent 2024–2026 study was found validating misconception-label accuracy or durable gains from method adaptation for Omega, Lemonæ, AdaptEd, or StarSpark.
- No product reviewed publishes randomized or within-learner evidence that switching among visual, story, worked-example, Socratic, or manipulative approaches improves delayed retention versus simply assigning more practice.
- Delayed retention and far-transfer results are generally absent from parent dashboards; “mastery” is usually based on immediate or course-end accuracy.
- The platforms do not publicly discuss uncertainty-aware mastery, forgetting curves at the individual skill level, or how false confidence and lucky guessing are handled.
- Public claims frequently blur “adaptive difficulty,” “personalized content,” “multi-modal instruction,” and “teaching-method adaptation.” These should be reported as separate capabilities unless a vendor documents the causal decision rule connecting learner signals to a changed instructional method.
